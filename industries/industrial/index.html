Industry · Manufacturing & Industrial

Intelligence that runs at the machine

Industrial environments have the least reliable connectivity and the most immediate consequences. AI that depends on a round trip to a datacentre is the wrong shape for the problem.

The pressure

What makes this sector different

Connectivity is not guaranteed

Plant floors, remote sites and moving assets lose connectivity routinely. A hosted call that fails is a capability that was never really there.

Latency has physical consequences

A decision arriving late is often the same as no decision when machinery is involved.

Hardware is constrained

Edge devices have modest processors and no GPU. Most AI tooling assumes otherwise.

How it gets used

Concrete applications

Inference on constrained hardware

NC AI is designed for CPU execution with a model measured in tens of megabytes, so it can run on the hardware already deployed rather than requiring an accelerator.

Works while disconnected

Inference runs locally with no account and no outbound dependency, so the capability survives a network outage instead of disappearing with it.

One artefact to deploy and roll back

NC compiles to a single binary with no runtime dependencies — a meaningful advantage when updating hundreds of devices you cannot easily reach.

Equipment and infrastructure monitoring

HiveANT investigates anomalies across many signals in parallel, with a digital twin used to predict the effect of an intervention before it is made.

Constraints we take seriously

What we will not claim

We have no industrial deployment and no device certification. We have not tested against industrial control protocols or safety standards. Anything touching safety-critical machinery needs engineering assessment far beyond a software evaluation, and we would not suggest otherwise.
